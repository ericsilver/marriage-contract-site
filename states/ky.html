Kentucky

Your marriage contract, Kentucky edition

Luck of the Draw score: 85 out of 100

Kentucky leaves property division, maintenance and fees to the judge's discretion. Fault is excluded from property division but may raise or lower maintenance. On the book's scale, 0 is a contract you can read and 100 is one that mostly says “ask the judge.” Kentucky ranks 36 of 51, counting from the state that leaves the least to the judge. The property system

Kentucky is one of forty-one equitable distribution jurisdictions. Equitable distribution. Each of you owns what's in your own name during the marriage. At a divorce a judge divides the marital property as the judge finds equitable, which is not a promise of half. Chapter 11 of the book covers what that means, and Chapter 22 covers what happens to it when you move.

Your spouse's medical bills (Article 5)

By statute a husband is liable for necessaries furnished to his wife after marriage, while a wife is not made liable for her husband's; the gender-based rule has been challenged but no court has ruled on its constitutionality.
KRS 404.040; White v. Universal Fidelity, LP, No. 3:17-cv-00044-GFVT (E.D. Ky. Oct. 19, 2018) (declining to reach the constitutional question)

Chapter 8 of the book explains the three ways states handle this.

If one of you dies (Article 9)

A surviving spouse who renounces the will takes the dower share: one-third of real estate the decedent owned at death, a life estate in one-third of real estate owned during the marriage but not at death (unless relinquished), and one-half of the surplus personal property. From July 15, 2026, surplus personalty and surplus real estate include payable-on-death and transfer-on-death assets, beneficiary-designated retirement accounts, joint-survivorship property, revocable-trust property, and transfers made within two years of death, but not life insurance.
KRS 392.080(1)(a); KRS 392.020 as amended by 2026 Ky. Acts ch. 134 (SB 50), § 2

Chapter 9 covers what a will can and can't take away from a spouse.

Prenups and postnups (Article 12)

Two looks, under case law only (Gentry); Kentucky has not adopted the UPAA or UPMAA. The court asks whether the agreement was obtained through fraud, duress, mistake, misrepresentation or nondisclosure of material facts; whether it is unconscionable; and whether facts and circumstances have changed since execution so as to make enforcement unfair and unreasonable.
Gentry v. Gentry, 798 S.W.2d 928, 936 (Ky. 1990), as quoted in Pamela Ford B. v. David Stanley B. (Ky. Ct. App. 2001)

What an agreement needs. No statute sets premarital-agreement formalities; the statute of frauds requires an agreement made in consideration of marriage to be in writing and signed by the party to be charged, and Gentry requires full disclosure of material facts. No notarization, witnesses, waiting period or independent counsel is required by statute.
KRS 371.010(5); Gentry v. Gentry, 798 S.W.2d 928 (Ky. 1990)
