Washington

Your marriage contract, Washington edition

Luck of the Draw score: 70 out of 100

Washington lets the judge divide all property of either spouse, including separate property, and set maintenance amount and length without a formula. Fault is excluded from both, and each party may remove the assigned judge once without cause. On the book's scale, 0 is a contract you can read and 100 is one that mostly says “ask the judge.” Washington ranks 18 of 51, counting from the state that leaves the least to the judge. The property system

Washington is one of nine community property jurisdictions. Community property. What either of you earns during the marriage belongs to both of you as it's earned, and it's generally split in half at the end. Chapter 11 of the book covers what that means, and Chapter 22 covers what happens to it when you move.

Your spouse's medical bills (Article 5)

Both of you can be liable from the first dollar. Family expenses and the education of the children, including stepchildren, are chargeable on the property of both spouses or either of them, and the spouses may be sued jointly or separately. In this community property state, community property is also available for community debts.
RCW 26.16.205

Chapter 8 of the book explains the three ways states handle this.

If one of you dies (Article 9)

Washington has no elective share. One-half of the community property is confirmed to the surviving spouse and the decedent may dispose of only the other half; one-half of quasi-community property (property acquired while domiciled elsewhere that would have been community property here) also belongs to the survivor.
RCW 11.02.070; RCW 26.16.030(1); RCW 26.16.220, 26.16.230

Prenups and postnups (Article 12)

One look, under case law only. A court first asks whether the agreement is substantively fair to the spouse opposing it; if it is not, the agreement is enforced only if it is procedurally fair, meaning full disclosure of the amount, character and value of the property and entry into the agreement freely, on independent advice of counsel, with full knowledge of rights. Fairness is judged at execution, not at enforcement.
In re Marriage of Bernard, 165 Wn.2d 895 (2009); In re Marriage of Matson, 107 Wn.2d 479, 730 P.2d 668 (1986)

What an agreement needs. An agreement made upon consideration of marriage must be in writing and signed by the party to be charged. No statute requires notarization, witnesses or a waiting period for a prenup, but an agreement that is not substantively fair survives only with full disclosure and independent advice of counsel. A community property agreement taking effect at death must be in writing under both spouses' hands and seals and witnessed and acknowledged like a deed.
RCW 19.36.010(3); In re Marriage of Bernard, 165 Wn.2d 895 (2009); RCW 26.16.120
