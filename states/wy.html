Wyoming

Your marriage contract, Wyoming edition

Luck of the Draw score: 90 out of 100

Wyoming leaves both property division, which can reach separately owned property, and alimony to the judge's sense of what is just, with fault allowed in both. A party may remove the assigned judge once without cause. On the book's scale, 0 is a contract you can read and 100 is one that mostly says “ask the judge.” Wyoming ranks 42 of 51, counting from the state that leaves the least to the judge. The property system

Wyoming is one of forty-one equitable distribution jurisdictions. Equitable distribution. Each of you owns what's in your own name during the marriage. At a divorce a judge divides the marital property as the judge finds equitable, which is not a promise of half. Chapter 11 of the book covers what that means, and Chapter 22 covers what happens to it when you move.

Your spouse's medical bills (Article 5)

Both of you can be liable from the first dollar. The necessary expenses of the family and the education of the children are chargeable on the property of both husband and wife, or either of them, and they may be sued jointly or separately; a spouse is not liable for the other's debts contracted before marriage without a written assumption.
Wyo. Stat. §§ 20-1-201, 20-1-202(e)

Chapter 8 of the book explains the three ways states handle this.

If one of you dies (Article 9)

A surviving spouse whom the will deprives of more than the elective share may elect one-half of the property subject to disposition under the will (after funeral and administration expenses, allowances and claims), or one-fourth if the survivor is not a parent of any of the decedent's surviving issue; the share reaches only the probate estate.
Wyo. Stat. § 2-5-101

Chapter 9 covers what a will can and can't take away from a spouse.

Prenups and postnups (Article 12)

Wyoming treats antenuptial agreements as contracts governed by the ordinary rules of contract construction, and a divorce court must enforce them unless it states a cogent reason for refusing. No Wyoming decision opened adopts a changed-circumstances review at divorce.
Lund v. Lund, 849 P.2d 731 (Wyo. 1993); Bradley v. Bradley, No. 04-139 (Wyo. 2005) (not yet checked against the source)

What an agreement needs. An agreement made upon consideration of marriage is void unless it or a memorandum is in writing and signed by the party to be charged. A waiver of the elective share, homestead allowance, exempt property and family allowance, before or after marriage, must be a written contract or waiver signed by the waiving party after fair disclosure. No statute requires notarization, witnesses, a waiting period or independent counsel.
Wyo. Stat. §§ 1-23-105(a)(iii), 2-5-102
