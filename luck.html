The Luck of the Draw Index

How much of the money each state leaves to the judge

Every state's marriage contract leaves some decisions to a judge, in words like equitable and just. States differ a great deal in how many, and the pattern isn't what you'd guess from a state's politics. Chapter 3 of the book describes the index. This page is the index itself.

Each state gets a score from 0 to 100. A state near 0 has a contract you can read. A state near 100 has a contract that mostly says “ask the judge.”

0–19 20–39 40–59 60–79 80–100

How the score is built

Five parts, each worth up to 20 points. More points means more is left to the judge.

  1. Property. Is property divided by a rule or by the judge's sense of fairness?
  2. Alimony. Is there a formula or a cap on amount and duration?
  3. Fault. Can marital misconduct move money?
  4. Lawyers' fees. How freely can the judge make one spouse pay the other's lawyer?
  5. Second opinion. Can you remove the assigned judge without cause, or ask for a jury?

Property scores 0 where the statute requires an equal split, 5 where there is a presumption of equal division, 10 where there is that presumption but the judge can also reach property owned before the marriage, 15 where the judge divides marital property with no presumption, and 20 where the judge can also reach property owned before the marriage or inherited. Alimony scores up to 10 for the amount and 10 for the duration: 0 for a formula or a hard cap, 5 for an advisory guideline or a presumptive limit, 10 for a list of factors. Fault scores up to 10 for property and 10 for alimony. Lawyers' fees score 0 if each side pays its own, 10 if a statute or the state's highest court makes an award presumptive or mandatory to keep the spouses on even terms, and 20 for general discretion, including need-based awards the judge may or may not make. Second opinion starts at 20 and loses 10 if you can remove the assigned judge once without cause and 10 if you can have a jury decide the money.

The index scores what the statutes and each state's highest court say, not what trial judges do in practice. Equal weights for the five parts are a choice, not a finding. Arbitration by agreement is available almost everywhere and isn't scored. Scores are checked against primary sources as of September 2026; an asterisk marks a state with a part not yet checked against its source. No lawyer has reviewed them.
