Where to Turn

If you aren’t safe, if you need legal help, and the government sources worth knowing.

From the book: Appendix E and Chapter 17.

This is a short list of organizations. They were chosen because they’re national and free or low-cost, and because they’re likely to still exist when you read this. Web addresses change, so search for the organization by name.

If you aren’t safe

National Domestic Violence Hotline. Call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788, or chat at thehotline.org. It’s open around the clock. Advocates there can help with safety planning. That includes the financial side, along with pets and the question of leaving the state with children.

Unchained At Last helps people who are facing a forced marriage or trying to leave one. That includes people who were married as minors.

Eldercare Locator (1-800-677-1116) connects you to local adult protective services. Call it if you suspect that an older relative is being exploited, including through a sudden marriage.

If there has been abuse and there are children

Most states have a legal presumption against awarding custody to a parent who has committed domestic violence. The presumption only helps if the court knows about the violence. Victims often don’t report it, often have no documentation, and are often advised by well-meaning friends not to “make it ugly.”

If there has been abuse, tell your lawyer at the first meeting. Keep records of dates, photographs, messages, and medical visits, and store them somewhere safe. Ask about a protective order. Chapter 1 covers moving children across state lines, and it is worth reading before you do. Leaving the state with the children without a court order can backfire badly, even when you’re leaving for good reason. Emergency jurisdiction exists for situations like this, and an advocate can help you use it.

You should also know the tactic that gets used in reverse. A parent accused of abuse sometimes answers by accusing the other parent of turning the children against them. Courts have at times credited that counter-accusation too readily. A lawyer experienced in domestic violence cases will know how your local judges handle it.

Call 1-800-799-7233, the National Domestic Violence Hotline. It can connect you with legal advocates who know your state’s courts.

Finding legal help

Your state bar’s lawyer referral service. Most of these services offer a reduced-fee first consultation. That’s enough for the “get the number” hour in Chapter 2.

LawHelp.org lists free civil legal aid by state. Eligibility depends on income, and cases involving children or abuse get priority.

Your court’s self-help center. Most family courts have one. It will have the forms and instructions, and sometimes a staff attorney who can look over your paperwork.

Limited-scope representation. Many states allow you to hire a lawyer for a single task, such as reviewing a settlement or drafting one agreement. Ask any lawyer whether they offer it.

American Academy of Matrimonial Lawyers (AAML) has a directory of experienced family lawyers. It’s most useful when the finances are complicated.

National Academy of Elder Law Attorneys (NAELA) is the place to look for planning around long-term care and Medicaid before a late-life marriage (Chapter 21).

Working it out without a courtroom

Academy of Professional Family Mediators and your state’s mediation association have mediator directories. A mediator can help a couple draft a prenup or postnup together, as well as a divorce settlement. Each of you still needs your own lawyer to review it.

International Academy of Collaborative Professionals (IACP) has a directory for collaborative divorce. In a collaborative divorce, both lawyers agree in advance to withdraw if the case goes to court.

Government sources worth knowing

Social Security Administration, ssa.gov, has the rules and calculators for benefits for spouses, divorced spouses, and survivors.

IRS Form 8857 is the request for innocent spouse relief. IRS Form 8379 is the injured spouse allocation (Chapter 8).

AnnualCreditReport.com is the only federally authorized source for your free credit reports.

Your county clerk or recorder is the office to call to confirm that your marriage license was actually filed (Chapter 5).

U.S. Department of State, Office of Children’s Issues handles international parental child abduction (Chapter 23).

If you’re in danger, none of the rest of this site matters yet. Call 1-800-799-7233 first.
