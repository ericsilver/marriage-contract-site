The Fifteen-Minute Audit

Who is actually named on every retirement account and life insurance policy, and the two moments when it matters most.

From the book: Chapters 9, 10 and 36.

If the two of you do only one thing because of this book, this should be it. It doesn’t require a lawyer, and it involves no disagreement and no imagining the marriage ending.

Every nightmare in this chapter happened to someone who assumed the form was fine and never checked it.

Nightmare Divorce Warren Hillman named his wife, Judy, on his federal employees’ life insurance in 1996. They divorced in 1998. He married Jacqueline in 2002 and died in 2008 without changing the form. Virginia law let the widow sue the ex-wife for the money. In 2013 the Supreme Court said federal law overrides that. Judy kept $124,558, and Jacqueline, his wife of six years, got nothing. Changing the form would have taken the fifteen minutes this job takes.

The day you file, update everything

The problem. You’ve filed for divorce, and you and your spouse are no longer speaking. Your will still leaves them everything, and your retirement account names them as beneficiary. If you’re hit by a bus on the way out of the courthouse, they get all of it, and they plan the service.

What you can do right away. Write a new will. It can’t take your spouse below the elective share, but it can take them down from 100 percent to the minimum. Sign a new health care proxy and power of attorney, and name someone else. Write down who you want in charge of your funeral.

What you may not be able to do. Many states freeze both spouses’ finances automatically when a divorce is filed. The freeze often covers changing the beneficiary on life insurance and retirement accounts. If your state has such an order, changing a beneficiary can put you in contempt of court. Ask your lawyer before you touch a beneficiary form. Wills are usually exempt from the freeze.

If you’re asked to sign the waiver

At some point, usually when your spouse retires, you may be handed a form and asked to sign it in front of a notary. It will be presented as routine. It will also be the most important financial document you sign in your sixties.

The form waives your survivor benefit. In return, your spouse’s monthly pension check gets bigger for as long as they live, often by 10 to 15 percent. When they die the checks stop completely. If you outlive them by twenty years, you’ll go twenty years with no pension income.

There are sometimes good reasons to sign. You may have a substantial pension of your own. You may be much older than your spouse, or in much worse health. Or the two of you may be buying life insurance with the difference, in which case you should check that the policy actually exists and can’t be cancelled.

You’re never required to sign. The law gave this decision to you, and deliberately not to your spouse, because too many people in your position had been left with nothing. If you feel pressured, that’s a reason to take the form to a fee-only financial planner or a lawyer before you sign it, and not after.
