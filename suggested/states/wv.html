West Virginia

Your marriage contract, West Virginia edition

75 of 100 on the Luck of the Draw Index

All states

Luck of the Draw score: 75 out of 100

West Virginia presumes an equal split of marital property and bars fault from the property decision. Spousal support amount and length are left to the judge’s weighing of factors, and the judge must compare the spouses’ fault when deciding support. On the book’s scale, 0 is a contract you can read and 100 is one that mostly says “ask the judge.” West Virginia ranks 25 of 51, counting from the state that leaves the least to the judge. The property system

West Virginia is one of forty-one equitable distribution jurisdictions. Equitable distribution. Each of you owns what’s in your own name during the marriage. At a divorce a judge divides the marital property as the judge finds equitable, which is not a promise of half. Chapter 11 of the book covers what that means, and Chapter 22 covers what happens to it when you move.

Your spouse’s medical bills (Article 5)

Both of you can be liable from the first dollar. A husband and wife are both liable for reasonable and necessary physician services rendered to either while they reside together, for the rent of the family residence, for articles purchased by either for the support of the family or the joint benefit of both, and for the support of a spouse and child while abandoned by the other.
W. Va. Code § 48-29-303

Chapter 8 of the book explains the three ways states handle this.

If one of you dies (Article 9)

The surviving spouse may elect a percentage of the augmented estate (probate estate plus the ’reclaimable estate’ of non-probate transfers, and the survivor’s own assets) that rises with the length of the marriage, from 3 percent after one year to 50 percent after 15 years, with a $25,000 supplemental amount.
W. Va. Code §§ 42-3-1, 42-3-2

Prenups and postnups (Article 12)

Two looks, under UPAA (1983) as enacted in 2023 with UPMAA-style additions, W. Va. Code §§ 48-1A-101 to 48-1A-1101; case law (Gant v. Gant) for agreements signed before July 1, 2023. An agreement signed on or after July 1, 2023 is unenforceable if not signed voluntarily, if either party was under 18 at marriage, or if unconscionable when signed with inadequate disclosure; in addition a court may refuse to enforce a term that was unconscionable at signing or whose enforcement would cause substantial hardship because of a material change in circumstances after signing. Older agreements are presumptively valid under Gant; the challenger must prove a problem in how the agreement was procured, and independent counsel is not required if the terms are understandable and both parties had the opportunity to consult counsel.
W. Va. Code §§ 48-1A-601, 48-1A-1001(b); Gant v. Gant, 174 W. Va. 740, 329 S.E.2d 106 (1985); Ware v. Ware, 224 W. Va. 599, 687 S.E.2d 382 (2009)

What an agreement needs. A premarital agreement must be in writing, signed by both parties, and contain an acknowledgment that both parties had an opportunity to consult separate legal counsel; it needs no consideration. Adequate disclosure means a reasonably accurate description and good-faith estimate of property, liabilities and income, a separate signed waiver, or adequate knowledge. If a spousal support waiver would make a party eligible for public assistance at separation or divorce, the family court may order support to avoid that eligibility; terms that affect child support, limit domestic-violence remedies, change divorce grounds or penalize filing for divorce are unenforceable.
W. Va. Code §§ 48-1A-201, 48-1A-601(b)-(c), 48-1A-701
