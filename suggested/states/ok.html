Oklahoma

Your marriage contract, Oklahoma edition

85 of 100 on the Luck of the Draw Index

All states

Luck of the Draw score: 85 out of 100

Oklahoma’s statute tells judges only to divide jointly acquired property justly and reasonably and to award alimony they think reasonable, so nearly everything is left to the judge. Premarital property stays with its owner, and fee awards follow what is just and proper. On the book’s scale, 0 is a contract you can read and 100 is one that mostly says “ask the judge.” Oklahoma ranks 36 of 51, counting from the state that leaves the least to the judge. Getting in

No wait from license to wedding. Both people apply under oath, in person, before a district court clerk, and each must be at least 18; since November 1, 2026, Oklahoma has no exception for minors. There is no waiting period. The license lasts 30 days, and the ceremony must occur within that time. A four-hour premarital counseling program lowers the fee from $50 to $5.
43 O.S. § 3, as amended by 2026 Okla. S.B. 504 (eff. Nov. 1, 2026); 43 O.S. §§ 5, 5.1, 20; 28 O.S. § 31

Who can marry you. A judge or retired judge, or an ordained or authorized minister, priest, rabbi or other religious leader at least 18 years old, may officiate before two adult witnesses. Friends (Quakers), Baha’is and Latter-day Saints may marry under their own practices without an ordained minister. The statute does not address online ordination; clergy certify that their church authorized them.
43 O.S. §§ 7, 8

Common-law marriage: A couple can still become married here without a license. A couple can still form a common-law marriage in Oklahoma. The person claiming one must show by clear and convincing evidence that both agreed to be married; living together and presenting themselves as spouses are evidence of that agreement. A 2020 appellate decision held the license statutes did not abolish it. The sources read do not address one formed in another state.
Standefer v. Standefer, 2001 OK 37, ¶ 11, 26 P.3d 104; In re Estate of Whitehouse, 2020 OK CIV APP 59, 479 P.3d 230

The property system

Oklahoma is one of forty-one equitable distribution jurisdictions. Equitable distribution. Each of you owns what’s in your own name during the marriage. At a divorce a judge divides the marital property as the judge finds equitable, which is not a promise of half. Chapter 11 of the book covers what that means, and Chapter 22 covers what happens to it when you move.

What counts as marital. The judge divides property the spouses acquired jointly during the marriage, whoever holds title, in a just and reasonable way; property acquired during the marriage is presumed joint. Premarital property, gifts and inheritances kept separate stay separate. The judge sets the cut-off date case by case. Growth on separate property is divisible only if a spouse’s efforts, skills or funds caused it.
43 O.S. § 121(B); Thielenhaus v. Thielenhaus, 1995 OK 5, 890 P.2d 925; Standefer v. Standefer, 2001 OK 37, ¶ 15; Payton v. Applegate (In re Estate of Applegate), 2024 OK 41, ¶ 6, 555 P.3d 1230

An inheritance that touches a joint account

Putting it in joint names usually makes it marital. An inheritance keeps its separate status only if it stays uncommingled. Moving separate money into a joint account is presumed a gift to the marriage, and the owner must overcome that with clear and convincing evidence of another purpose. Money in a joint checking account ordinarily loses its separate identity unless specific funds can be traced.
In re Marriage of Cotton, 2023 OK CIV APP 21, ¶¶ 9, 12, 19-21, 530 P.3d 872; Payton v. Applegate, 2024 OK 41, ¶¶ 6-7, 10, 555 P.3d 1230; Wigley v. Skelton, 1978 OK 26, as quoted in Cotton

Your spouse’s medical bills (Article 5)

Both of you can be liable from the first dollar. Spouses are jointly and severally liable for debts incurred for necessaries furnished to either spouse, unless a law or court order provides otherwise.
Okla. Stat. tit. 43, § 209.1 (added 2000)

Chapter 8 of the book explains the three ways states handle this.

If one of you dies (Article 9)

For decedents dying on or after July 1, 1985, a will may not leave the surviving spouse less than an undivided one-half interest in property acquired by the joint industry of the spouses during the marriage, and the spouse may elect that one-half in place of the will’s gifts; the statute operates against the will and does not by its terms reach non-probate transfers.
Okla. Stat. tit. 84, § 44(B)

Prenups and postnups (Article 12)

One look, under case law only (no UPAA); statutes recognize a ’valid antenuptial contract in writing’. An antenuptial agreement that is just and reasonable and entered freely without fraud is enforced, including a waiver of alimony and property division on divorce; if the agreement is unfair on its face, the party relying on it must show full and fair disclosure and good faith. Postnuptial agreements that alter divorce property rights, including post-marriage amendments to a prenup, have been held unenforceable because no statute authorizes them.
Hudson v. Hudson, 350 P.2d 596 (Okla. 1960); Freeman v. Freeman, 565 P.2d 365 (Okla. 1977); In re Estate of Cobb, 305 P.2d 1028 (Okla. 1956); Hendrick v. Hendrick, 1999 OK CIV APP 15, 976 P.2d 1071

What an agreement needs. The agreement must be in writing to control property division on divorce or to limit a will; no statute requires notarization, witnesses, a waiting period or independent counsel. Disclosure matters because an agreement that looks unfair shifts to its proponent the burden of proving full disclosure; alimony waivers have been enforced.
Okla. Stat. tit. 43, § 121(B); Okla. Stat. tit. 84, § 44; Hudson v. Hudson, 350 P.2d 596 (Okla. 1960); In re Estate of Cobb, 305 P.2d 1028 (Okla. 1956)

Children (Article 8)

Child support: income shares. Oklahoma computes support as a percentage of both parents’ combined gross income, then divides it by each parent’s share of that income, which is an income shares model. Support runs to 18, or for a full-time high school student to graduation or age 20, whichever comes first. A disabled child’s support can be indefinite. Title 43 has no college provision; parents may agree to more.
43 O.S. §§ 112(E), 112.1A, 118D; Holleyman v. Holleyman, 2003 OK 48, 78 P.3d 921

Moving away with a child. A custodial parent must mail 60 days’ notice before moving a child more than 75 miles, or within 10 days of learning of the move if earlier notice was impossible. The other parent has 30 days to file an objection, or the move is allowed. The moving parent must show good faith; the objecting parent must then show the move is not in the child’s best interest.
43 O.S. § 112.3(A), (C), (G), (K); 43 O.S. § 112.2A

Getting out (Article 2)

How long you must live here before filing: six months. Either spouse must have lived in Oklahoma in good faith for six months immediately before filing, so a newcomer whose spouse lives elsewhere must wait six months. The filer may file in the county where they have lived for 30 days, or in the respondent’s county. Where the marriage took place does not change the requirement.
43 O.S. §§ 102, 103(A)(1), 131

How long it takes. Either spouse can seek a divorce for incompatibility, with no separation period, and the other spouse’s opposition alone cannot stop it. The judge must still find actual incompatibility from objective evidence. With minor children, no final order may issue for 90 days after filing unless the court waives that for good cause without objection, or after counseling. Without minor children, no statute sets a wait.
43 O.S. §§ 101 (Seventh), 107.1; Hughes v. Hughes, 1961 OK 112, 363 P.2d 155; Williams v. Williams, 1975 OK 163, 543 P.2d 1401

The legal minimum for a spouse who moves here alone and files as soon as allowed, with no minor children and the other spouse objecting, is about 180 days from arrival to a final decree (180 days of residence with no minor children; 180 + 90 = 270 with minor children). Courts often take longer.
