Utah

Your marriage contract, Utah edition

70 of 100 on the Luck of the Draw Index

All states

Luck of the Draw score: 70 out of 100

Utah starts property division from a presumption of equal shares of marital property, and limits alimony to the length of the marriage unless the judge finds good cause. The judge may weigh defined kinds of fault when setting alimony and has broad discretion over attorney fees. On the book’s scale, 0 is a contract you can read and 100 is one that mostly says “ask the judge.” Utah ranks 18 of 51, counting from the state that leaves the least to the judge. The property system

Utah is one of forty-one equitable distribution jurisdictions. Equitable distribution. Each of you owns what’s in your own name during the marriage. At a divorce a judge divides the marital property as the judge finds equitable, which is not a promise of half. Chapter 11 of the book covers what that means, and Chapter 22 covers what happens to it when you move.

Your spouse’s medical bills (Article 5)

Both of you can be liable from the first dollar. A married person and his or her property are chargeable for family expenses (expenses that benefit and promote the family unit) and for a minor child’s education, and may be sued separately or jointly with the spouse; where one spouse signed a written agreement, the creditor may recover the contract amounts from both spouses jointly and severally.
Utah Code § 81-3-109 (formerly § 30-2-9)

Chapter 8 of the book explains the three ways states handle this.

If one of you dies (Article 9)

The surviving spouse may elect one-third of the augmented estate (net probate estate, non-probate transfers to others and to the spouse, and the spouse’s own property), with a $75,000 supplemental amount; the homestead, exempt-property and family allowances are charged against the share.
Utah Code §§ 75-2-202, 75-2-203

Chapter 9 covers what a will can and can’t take away from a spouse.

Prenups and postnups (Article 12)

One look, under UPAA (1983), Utah Code §§ 81-3-201 to 81-3-208 (formerly ch. 30-8). A premarital agreement is unenforceable only if the challenger proves he or she did not sign voluntarily, or that it was fraudulent when signed and the challenger received no reasonable disclosure, did not waive disclosure in writing, and had no adequate knowledge of the other’s finances. Utah’s version uses ’fraudulent’ where the uniform act says ’unconscionable’.
Utah Code § 81-3-205

What an agreement needs. A premarital agreement must be in writing and signed by both parties and needs no consideration; the statute does not require notarization, witnesses, a waiting period or independent counsel. If a spousal support waiver would leave a spouse eligible for public assistance at separation or divorce, the court may order support to the extent needed to avoid that eligibility. The agreement cannot affect a child’s right to support, health and medical expenses, medical insurance or child care coverage.
Utah Code §§ 81-3-202, 81-3-203, 81-3-205(2)
