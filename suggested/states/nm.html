New Mexico

Your marriage contract, New Mexico edition

50 of 100 on the Luck of the Draw Index

All states

Luck of the Draw score: 50 out of 100

New Mexico splits community property equally and leaves out marital fault, so the judge has little room on property. The judge has broad discretion over spousal support, and each side can excuse one assigned judge without giving a reason. On the book’s scale, 0 is a contract you can read and 100 is one that mostly says “ask the judge.” New Mexico ranks 5 of 51, counting from the state that leaves the least to the judge. The property system

New Mexico is one of nine community property jurisdictions. Community property. What either of you earns during the marriage belongs to both of you as it’s earned, and it’s generally split in half at the end. Chapter 11 of the book covers what that means, and Chapter 22 covers what happens to it when you move.

Your spouse’s medical bills (Article 5)

Both of you can be liable from the first dollar. New Mexico is a community property state: a debt either spouse incurs during marriage, including a medical bill, is a community debt unless it falls in a statutory separate-debt category, and it is paid first from all community property and then the residence; the other spouse’s separate property is not liable unless that spouse also incurred the debt.
NMSA 1978, §§ 40-3-9, 40-3-11

Chapter 8 of the book explains the three ways states handle this.

If one of you dies (Article 9)

New Mexico has no elective share. The surviving spouse already owns half of the community property; the decedent may will away only the decedent’s half, and without a will that half passes to the survivor. Quasi-community property is treated as community property only on divorce or legal separation, not at death.
NMSA 1978, §§ 45-2-102, 45-3-101, 40-3-8(D)

Chapter 9 covers what a will can and can’t take away from a spouse.

Prenups and postnups (Article 12)

One look, under UPAA (1983). A premarital agreement is unenforceable only if the challenger proves it was signed involuntarily, or was unconscionable when signed and the challenger lacked fair disclosure, did not waive disclosure in writing, and lacked adequate knowledge. Spouses may contract with each other about property, subject to the common-law rules for persons in a confidential relationship; outside property, they may contract only for an immediate separation and support during it.
NMSA 1978, §§ 40-3A-7, 40-2-2, 40-2-8

What an agreement needs. The agreement must be in writing, signed by both parties and acknowledged (notarized); the statute sets no waiting period and no counsel requirement, and disclosure matters through the enforcement test. A premarital agreement may not adversely affect a spouse’s right to support, so a waiver of alimony is not enforceable.
NMSA 1978, §§ 40-3A-3, 40-3A-4(B)
