Illinois

Your marriage contract, Illinois edition

35 of 100 on the Luck of the Draw Index

All states

Luck of the Draw score: 35 out of 100

Illinois leaves property division to the judge, but maintenance for most couples follows a statutory formula for both amount and length, and fault is excluded. Interim fee awards follow a parity rule, and each side can substitute the judge once without cause. On the book’s scale, 0 is a contract you can read and 100 is one that mostly says “ask the judge.” Illinois ranks 1 of 51, counting from the state that leaves the least to the judge. The property system

Illinois is one of forty-one equitable distribution jurisdictions. Equitable distribution. Each of you owns what’s in your own name during the marriage. At a divorce a judge divides the marital property as the judge finds equitable, which is not a promise of half. Chapter 11 of the book covers what that means, and Chapter 22 covers what happens to it when you move.

Your spouse’s medical bills (Article 5)

Both of you can be liable from the first dollar. The expenses of the family and of the children’s education are chargeable on the property of both spouses or either of them, and creditors may sue the spouses jointly or separately; hospital bills have been pursued under this family expense statute.
750 ILCS 65/15(a)(1); St. Mary of Nazareth Hospital v. Kuczaj, 174 Ill. App. 3d 268 (1988)

Chapter 8 of the book explains the three ways states handle this.

If one of you dies (Article 9)

A surviving spouse who renounces the will takes one-third of the entire estate if the decedent left a descendant, or one-half if not, after payment of claims; the share is measured against the probate estate, and assets in a revocable trust are not reached unless the trust is illusory or a fraud on marital rights.
755 ILCS 5/2-8; Johnson v. La Grange State Bank, 73 Ill. 2d 342 (1978)

Chapter 9 covers what a will can and can’t take away from a spouse.

Prenups and postnups (Article 12)

Two looks, under UPAA (1983), 750 ILCS 10, with a modified support clause. Property terms are tested only at signing (involuntary, or unconscionable when signed with inadequate disclosure), but if a support waiver causes undue hardship because of circumstances not reasonably foreseeable at signing, the court may order support anyway. Postmarital agreements are enforced as contracts that need consideration, and their unconscionability is judged by the parties’ circumstances immediately after signing.
750 ILCS 10/7(a), (b); In re Marriage of Tabassum, 377 Ill. App. 3d 761 (2007)

What an agreement needs. A premarital agreement must be in writing and signed by both parties and needs no consideration; no notarization, witnesses, waiting period or independent counsel is required, disclosure matters through the unconscionability defense, and a spousal-support waiver can be overridden for unforeseeable undue hardship.
750 ILCS 10/3, 10/7
