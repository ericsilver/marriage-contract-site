Tennessee

Your marriage contract, Tennessee edition

85 of 100 on the Luck of the Draw Index

All states

Luck of the Draw score: 85 out of 100

Tennessee leaves the property split and alimony amount and length to the judge’s weighing of factors, with no formula or cap. Fault is barred from property division but may be weighed for alimony. On the book’s scale, 0 is a contract you can read and 100 is one that mostly says “ask the judge.” Tennessee ranks 36 of 51, counting from the state that leaves the least to the judge. The property system

Tennessee is one of forty-one equitable distribution jurisdictions. Equitable distribution. Each of you owns what’s in your own name during the marriage. At a divorce a judge divides the marital property as the judge finds equitable, which is not a promise of half. Chapter 11 of the book covers what that means, and Chapter 22 covers what happens to it when you move.

Your spouse’s medical bills (Article 5)

Tennessee courts apply the common-law doctrine of necessaries to hold a spouse liable for necessary medical care provided to the other spouse; a 2015 bill to abolish the doctrine did not pass.
Out Patient Diagnostic Center v. Christian, No. 01A01-9510-CV-00467 (Tenn. Ct. App. Apr. 30, 1997); S.B. 1231, 109th Gen. Assembly (2015) (not enacted) (not yet checked against the source)

Chapter 8 of the book explains the three ways states handle this.

If one of you dies (Article 9)

The surviving spouse may elect a share of the net estate that depends on the length of the marriage: 10 percent under 3 years, 20 percent at 3 to 6 years, 30 percent at 6 to 9 years, and 40 percent at 9 years or more. The net estate is the probate estate less secured debts, expenses and allowances; a conveyance made fraudulently to defeat the share can be added back and set aside.
Tenn. Code Ann. § 31-4-101; Tenn. Code Ann. § 31-1-105

Prenups and postnups (Article 12)

One look, under own statute (Tenn. Code Ann. § 36-3-501) and case law. A prenuptial agreement binds the court if it was entered into freely, knowledgeably and in good faith, without duress or undue influence; ’knowledgeably’ means the enforcing spouse proves full and fair disclosure of his or her holdings or that the other spouse already knew them. An alimony waiver is enforced unless enforcement would make the waiving spouse a public charge. Postnuptial agreements are enforceable if supported by adequate consideration, knowingly entered, and free of fraud, coercion or duress.
Tenn. Code Ann. § 36-3-501; Randolph v. Randolph, 937 S.W.2d 815 (Tenn. 1996); Cary v. Cary, 937 S.W.2d 777 (Tenn. 1996); Bratton v. Bratton, 136 S.W.3d 595 (Tenn. 2004)

What an agreement needs. An agreement made upon consideration of marriage must be in writing and signed by the party to be charged; no statute requires notarization, witnesses or a waiting period, and Randolph did not require independent counsel. The enforcing spouse must prove full and fair disclosure or the other spouse’s independent knowledge. An alimony waiver yields if enforcement would make the waiving spouse a public charge.
Tenn. Code Ann. § 29-2-101(a)(3); Tenn. Code Ann. § 36-3-501; Randolph v. Randolph, 937 S.W.2d 815 (Tenn. 1996); Cary v. Cary, 937 S.W.2d 777 (Tenn. 1996)
